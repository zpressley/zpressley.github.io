Pantheon Leagues

A fantasy baseball platform I built from scratch: live draft and auction tooling, a league economy with its own currency and salary cap, and twelve years of league history carried off Yahoo Fantasy Sports without losing any of it.

Why build it

Off-the-shelf fantasy platforms assume every league plays the same way. Ours doesn't. We run a keeper format with its own currency, a salary cap, and rules that have piled up over a decade of play. Running it meant keeping a spreadsheet beside the platform, and eventually the spreadsheet was where the league actually lived.

Three separate tools grew out of that: a prospect database, a trade evaluator, and a draft app. Each one solved a piece of a season, and none of them talked to the others. The same player existed three times, in three shapes, with no shared truth about who he was or how he was performing.

What it is now

Now it's one Postgres-backed application where the player is the primary entity and everything else hangs off it: prospect grades, contract and salary state, draft board position, live season stats.

  • Live draft and auction tooling. Real-time bidding and pick management, with cap and roster validity enforced as picks land rather than reconciled afterward.
  • A league economy. A custom currency with a salary-cap calculator, so a trade's legality and its cap consequences are answerable at the moment it's proposed.
  • Twelve-plus years of history, migrated. Every season carried off Yahoo Fantasy Sports, which meant reconciling player identities across a decade of roster data, trades, and name changes into one identity per player.
  • Live data sync. Season stats stay current without re-ingesting the world on every run.

The interesting problem

Player identity. Sources don't agree on IDs, don't agree on naming, and a decade of archived league data agrees with neither. Nearly everything else in the system depends on getting that right: trade valuation, cap math, draft board position, historical records. It's the same problem I spend my working life on with accounts and contacts, except here I happen to know every record personally.
