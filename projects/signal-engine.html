Nonprofit Growth-Signal Engine

A GTM engineering take-home: design a system that finds every nonprofit actively hiring fundraising leadership, ranks them by real hiring intensity, and puts callable contacts in front of an AE — without spending enrichment credits on the records that were never going to qualify.

The ask

A sales leader wants every mid-to-large nonprofit currently hiring a Director of Development or a Development Operations role — found, scored by urgency, and routed to the right rep quickly. The interesting part isn't the search. It's that paid enrichment makes a naive version of this expensive: enrich everything you find, and most of the spend lands on organizations that were never going to clear the bar.

The shape of the problem

Data volume in this pipeline moves like a barbell. Many raw postings narrow to one row per company, get scored down to a small outlier shortlist, then fan back out to several contacts per flagged organization. Both pinch points are where duplicates get made — or killed.

  • The narrow neck (postings → companies). Every posting has to prove itself against the CRM before it can create or attach to a company record. Skip that and one org with three concurrent reqs becomes three duplicate accounts.
  • The wide end (companies → contacts). One flagged company surfaces several target-title contacts, and the same person can surface twice if more than one posting triggered the search. Contacts dedupe on email and LinkedIn URL, and match against the CRM, before any paid enrichment runs.

How it runs

Three phases, and each one spends money differently.

1 · Collect — sweep everything, spend nothing

A Claude Code scraper on a cron job works Idealist, the AFP Career Center, and state nonprofit association boards. A Cowork task parses LinkedIn saved-search alert emails out of a connected Gmail account. Everything lands in one deduped JSON ledger, matched on posting URL — or on organization plus title plus post date where no stable URL exists. Storage and comparison cost nothing per record, so this layer stays effectively free.

2 · Score — compare peers, not the whole population

Postings collapse on normalized domain into a Companies table, one row per organization regardless of how many concurrent reqs it has. Those companies split into employee-count tiers, and each tier is scored independently against a median and interquartile range. An organization whose concurrent postings exceed Q3 + 1.5 × IQR for its tier is hiring beyond what normal churn explains — that's the signal, and it scores a 5. Where a tier's sample is too thin for IQR to mean anything, percentile rank in bands of 20% stands in. Still no credits spent.

3 · Expand — now, and only now, money moves

A score of 5 fires Find Contacts at Company, searching target buyer titles rather than the requisition that tripped the flag. Results dedupe on email plus LinkedIn URL, then a CRM check decides the enrichment path: a recognized contact justifies the full paid cascade, a net-new contact gets the same cascade plus the basic attributes routing needs. Both branches converge on one outreach-ready record and route to a CRM campaign or a Gong Engage flow.

Decisions worth defending

  • Match to CRM before spending credits, at both the account and contact level. Clay writes to the CRM over API, which typically bypasses CRM dedupe rules on object creation. These checks are the dedupe — and they cut spend at the same time.
  • Score against peer tiers, not the raw population. A flat count of open postings just re-ranks the biggest organizations. It rewards size, not intent — the exact vanity metric the system exists to avoid.
  • Reserve paid discovery for the shortlist. Letting the enrichment platform find postings burns credits on records that mostly won't qualify. A scraper is nearly free and only needs a fix when a source site changes its HTML.
  • Normalize before you dedupe. Clay's built-in dedupe is exact-match only, so it won't catch "Acme Inc" against "Acme, Inc." Normalizing name and domain into a shared key is the load-bearing step — not the lookup that follows it.

Why the lead doesn't go stale

A specific requisition can fill or expire, but the organization already cleared the outlier bar. What's real and current is its demonstrated investment in fundraising capacity, not the literal job posting — and the target-title contacts remain callable people at a company that is spending on this problem right now. When a posting closes, refresh the talk track, not the lead.
