01 · The Ask

Every nonprofit hiring fundraising leadership — right now.

A sales leader wants every mid-to-large nonprofit actively hiring a Director of Development or Development Ops role:

STEP A

Found

Swept from job boards and LinkedIn alerts into one deduped ledger — nothing missed, nothing counted twice.

STEP B

Scored

Ranked by hiring urgency against true peers — not by who happens to be the biggest org in the list.

STEP C

Routed

Real, callable contacts delivered to the right AE via CRM campaign or Gong Engage — fast.

02 · The Shape of the System

Data moves like a barbell.

Many raw postings narrow to one row per company, get scored down to a small outlier shortlist, then fan back out to multiple contacts per flagged org. The two pinch points are where duplicates are made — or killed.

The Weeds · 1 of 3Postings Enrichment Waterfall

Every posting proves itself before it costs a credit.

1

Match CRM by company name FREE

Runs on every new row. Match found → attach signal to the existing Account, done. No match → step 2.

2

Clay Native Enrich 1 CREDIT

Only fires when step 1 misses. Returns domain, normalized name, LinkedIn URL, employee count — real match keys instead of a fuzzy string.

3

Retry CRM match FREE

Cascades domain → LinkedIn URL → name. Match → attach to Account. Miss → flag for new-Account creation.

WHY STEP 3 EXISTS

A name-only match misses abbreviations, punctuation, and “Inc.”-style variants. Domain and LinkedIn URL are far more reliable keys — the retry isn't working from a fuzzy string alone.

MECHANICS NOTES

Clay's built-in dedupe is exact-match only — normalization into a shared key is the load-bearing step, not the lookup. Webhook sources cap at 50k rows/table: a non-issue at this volume, flagged if sources expand.

The Weeds · 2 of 3The Scoring Table

Statistical outliers, not raw counts.

PRIMARY

Median + IQR per tier

Flag orgs whose concurrent postings exceed Q3 + 1.5×IQR for their tier. Flagged = automatic score 5.

FALLBACK

Percentile rank

When a tier's sample is too thin for IQR, rank by percentile in bands of 20% → scores 1–5.

TRIGGER

Score 5 → find contacts

Only a 5 fires Find Contacts at Company. Two counts tracked separately: Dir. of Development and Dev Ops postings.

The Weeds · 3 of 3The Contacts Table

Dedupe the person before enriching the person.

Raw results land, dedupe immediately on email + LinkedIn URL, then a CRM check decides the enrichment path:

BRANCH 1 · CRM MATCH

Full paid cascade

LinkedIn → email waterfall → direct phone. A recognized contact justifies the full spend: confirmed profile, verified email, dial-ready number.

BRANCH 2 · NET NEW

Cascade + routing data

Same cascade, plus basic enrich — location, title, seniority — so a net-new contact carries what routing needs.

BRANCH 3 · RETRY

Re-match with more data

Cascades email → LinkedIn → name. Match → attach to existing contact. Miss → flag for new contact/lead creation.

Why the repeated CRM checks? Clay writes to the CRM over API — which typically bypasses CRM dedupe rules on object creation. Matching here is the single rule keeping duplicate Accounts and Contacts out of Salesforce, and it lowers enrichment cost. Both branches converge into one outreach-ready record → CRM Campaign or Gong Engage.

06 · Design Rationale

The objections, answered.

“WHY NOT SCORE BY NUMBER OF OPENINGS?”

A raw count just re-ranks the biggest organizations. It rewards size, not intent — exactly the vanity metric this system exists to avoid.

“WHY NOT HAVE CLAY FIND THE POSTINGS?”

Discovery at scale burns paid credits on records that mostly won't clear the bar. A scraper is nearly free — it needs an occasional Claude Code fix when a site's HTML shifts.

“WHY SO MANY CRM CHECKS?”

API writes typically ignore CRM dedupe rules on creation. Checks are the dedupe — and they cut spend. The goal is finding an opportunity, not mass enrichment.

“WHAT IF THE POSTING CLOSES?”

The lead doesn't go stale. The org already cleared the outlier bar; its contacts are real, callable people at a company demonstrably investing in fundraising capacity. Re-check the posting before quoting it — refresh the talk track, not the lead.

07 · Bottom Line

One question, answered honestly:

Which nonprofits are investing in fundraising capacity right now, beyond what normal turnover explains — and who, specifically, should an AE call about it?

Everything after Collect exists to protect that answer from noise, duplication, and unnecessary spend.
